## LAUNCH STRATEGY

### Bulk Transport: Falcon Heavy

**Capacity:**
- LEO: 26.7 tons
- TLI (Trans-Lunar Injection): ~16 tons
- TMI (Trans-Mars Injection): ~13.5 tons

**Launch count:**
- 4,500 tons / 13.5 tons = ~333 launches (direct to Mars)
- 5-10 launches (optimal, details below)

**Cost:**
- Falcon Heavy: ~$100M/launch (full capacity)
- Total: $1-2B (launch only)

### Modular Packaging

**Problem:** Cannot launch 5 km diameter rings as one piece.

**Solution:** Divide rings into segments, local assembly.

**One ring (R=2500 m, 5 turns) segmentation:**
- 5° slices = 72 segments/ring
- 5 rings = 360 segments
- Each segment: 218 m wire, ~500 kg
- 360 launches needed (impossible)

**More realistic:**
- 30° slices = 12 segments/ring
- 5 rings = 60 segments
- Each segment: 1.5 km wire, ~3 tons
- Package size: ~1 m diameter coil

**Local assembly robots:**
- 2-4 robots, each ~500 kg
- Combine 60 segments
- ~6-12 months duration

**Cost optimization:**
- 4,500 tons / 1 ton/segment = 4,500 segments
- 4,500 / 30 = 150 launches (if Starship used)
- Starship: 100+ tons to Mars = 45 launches

### Starship (SpaceX) — Better

**Capacity:**
- To Mars: ~100 tons/launch (estimate, 2030s)

**Scenario:**
- 4,500 tons / 100 tons = 45 launches
- Each launch: ~$50M (estimate)
- Total launch: $2.25B
- **This could be possible within 20-30 years**

## ASSEMBLY SCENARIOS

### Scenario A: In-Situ Assembly (Mars Surface)

**Advantages:**
- Gravity aid (structural support)
- Human-assisted assembly possible
- Easier repair/replacement

**Disadvantages:**
- 5 km diameter structure built on surface
- Rings are planar (curved surface difficulties)
- Dust storm risk

**Method:**
- Ring foundations (4-6 points) prepared
- Ring segments lifted by cranes
- Joined at welding/connection stations
- Current induced in situ

**Duration:** 2-3 years (parallel segment assembly)

### Scenario B: Orbital Assembly (Lagrange Point)

**Advantages:**
- Zero gravity, easier assembly
- Independent of Mars
- Orbital mechanics: topology is natural

**Disadvantages:**
- Mars transfer + landing additional
- Unmanned assembly (robotic)
- Difficult repair

**Method:**
- Modules assembled in Mars orbit
- As one piece slowly descended to Mars
- Deploys after surface touch

**Duration:** 3-5 years

### Scenario C: Hybrid

**Most realistic:**
- Ring segments sent to Mars surface
- Combined on surface by cranes
- Induced when ring is complete
- Base structures built inside ring (parallel)

**Advantage:** Most flexible, most realistic.

## INITIAL "CHARGE" PROCEDURE

**Problem:** Inducing 4,500-ton system for the first time requires large energy.

**Calculation:**
- Magnetic energy: 5,300 GJ
- Conversion efficiency: 80% (power source → current)
- Required energy: 6,625 GJ = 1,840 MWh
- 1 year at 1 MW continuous source: 1 year!

**Solution:**
- 1-2 year gradual charging
- Phase 1: Core solenoid (1,250 GJ) → 6 months
- Phase 2: Outer perimeter (1,266 GJ) → 6 months
- Phase 3: Middle layer (1.37 GJ) → 1 week

**Power source:**
- Mars: solar panels (50 kW continuous) or nuclear (100 kW)
- 2-year charging: 1 MWh × 4,500 = 4,500 MWh must be stored
- Battery: Li-ion 4,500 MWh = 1,125 tons (5× extra mass!)

**Better method:** Direct production (portable power sent from Earth + Mars solar panels):
- 1 year of slow charging
- Battery: 100 MWh (50 tons, sufficient)

## OPERATION

### Annual Maintenance

**On-site monitoring:**
- Current sensors (per ring)
- Magnetometers (inner zone, outer zone)
- Temperature sensors
- Vacuum/protection status

**Remote control:**
- Current correction (via resistive heating for local current reduction)
- Cryocooler control
- Emergency shutdown (quench detection)

### 5-10 Year Recharge

**Procedure:**
- When flux drops 10-20%
- Portable power source from Mars surface
- Slow "charge" of entire system for 1-2 weeks
- During this, shield is weak, base shelters

**Alternative: Continuous "trickle charge"**
- Small current (1-10 A) always added
- Flux pump technique
- Compensates for flux loss
- 5-10 year major recharge unnecessary

## RISKS

### Critical Risks

1. **Interstellar launch cost increase** (high): $5B → $20B
2. **Mars landing failure** (medium): 1-2 lost segments
3. **Local assembly failure** (medium): 1-2 year delay
4. **Initial charge problems** (medium): 6-12 month delay
5. **Structural damage (dust storm, meteor)** (medium): 1-2 year repair

### Mitigation

- Multiple launches, insurance
- Local backup segments
- Modular design (damaged segment isolated)
- 2-3 year buffer time
- Structural redundancy (double rings)
